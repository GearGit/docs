Response quality problems are common in any survey program. OnlineSurvey automatically flags three types of low-quality responses so you can identify and remove them before drawing conclusions. This page explains what each type means, how the platform detects them, and what you can do to reduce them.

## Types of Quality Issues

### Speeders

A speeder is a respondent who completes the survey significantly faster than the median completion time. Speed alone does not prove carelessness, but completing a 20-minute survey in 90 seconds is a strong signal that the respondent did not read the questions.

OnlineSurvey measures completion time for every response. If a respondent finishes in less than one-third of the median time for that survey, the response is flagged as a speeder and assigned a **Quality Reject** disposition in the results.

### Straight-Liners

Straight-lining happens when a respondent selects the same answer for every row in a grid or matrix question. For example, choosing "4" for every row of a 10-row rating grid. This pattern suggests the respondent was not reading individual items.

OnlineSurvey detects straight-lining on grid and matrix questions. A response is flagged when all rows in a grid receive identical answers and the grid has four or more rows.

### Drop-Off

Drop-off refers to respondents who start a survey but do not complete it. These are recorded as **Partial** responses. They are not flagged as quality rejects, but they are tracked separately from complete responses.

High drop-off rates at a specific question often indicate that the question is confusing, too sensitive, or comes too late in a long survey.

## How Quality Flags Appear in Results

Flagged responses appear in the **Responses** tab with a colored disposition badge.

| Disposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| Complete | Fully completed, no quality flags |
| Quality Reject | Flagged as speeder or straight-liner |
| Partial | Started but did not finish |
| Screen-Out | Failed a screening question |
| Over Quota | Quota cell was already full |

![The Responses tab showing a list of responses with disposition badges in different colors](/images/placeholder.png)

By default, the analysis charts and summary tables include all Complete responses and exclude Quality Rejects, Partials, and Screen-Outs. You can confirm or change this from the **Filter** panel in the analysis view.

## Filtering Out Quality Rejects

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your survey's results">
    Navigate to the survey and click the **Results** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Filter panel">
    Click **Filter** in the top-right area of the results view. The filter panel slides open on the right side.

    ![The results view with the Filter panel open on the right side](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the disposition filter">
    Under **Disposition**, ensure that only **Complete** is checked. Uncheck **Quality Reject** and **Partial** if they are selected. Click **Apply**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the filtered count">
    The response count in the top bar updates to reflect only the responses matching your filter. All charts and tables now exclude flagged responses.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Filters applied in the results view also apply to any insights report you generate from AI narration. Check your filter settings before generating a report to make sure you are narrating the right set of responses.
</Note>

## Design Practices That Reduce Quality Issues

Good survey design is the most effective way to reduce speeding, straight-lining, and drop-off before they happen.

**Keep surveys short.** Every additional question increases the chance of respondent fatigue. For most topics, 10 to 15 questions is the practical upper limit for high-quality responses. If your survey must be longer, use screen-outs to remove ineligible respondents early so qualified respondents spend less time on it overall.

**Vary question types.** A survey that uses only rating scales invites straight-lining. Mixing closed questions, open ends, and ranking questions forces respondents to engage differently with each section.

**Use logical screen-outs.** Place your most important qualifying questions at the beginning of the survey. Respondents who do not qualify are exited early, which reduces drop-off among in-scope respondents who can feel the survey is relevant to them throughout.

**Avoid unnecessary grid rows.** Long grids with many rows are the most common source of straight-lining. If a grid has more than eight rows, consider splitting it into two shorter grids or replacing some rows with individual questions.

**Set realistic length expectations.** If your survey takes longer than five minutes, tell respondents upfront. Unexpected length is a leading cause of mid-survey abandonment.

<Tip>
  You can see exactly where respondents are dropping off by scrolling through the **Drop-off** chart in the **Results > Overview** tab. Each question bar shows the percentage of respondents who exited at that point.
</Tip>
