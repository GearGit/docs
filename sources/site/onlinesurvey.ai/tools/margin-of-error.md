## About this tool

You’ve collected your survey responses, but how much should you trust the results? That depends on your margin of error. The margin of error tells you how far your survey results might deviate from the true value in the overall population. Our free Margin of Error Calculator gives you an instant answer: enter your sample size, population size, and confidence level, and you’ll know exactly how precise your data is.

A smaller margin of error means more reliable results, but it requires a larger sample. A larger margin of error is acceptable for informal surveys or early-stage research, but can be dangerous for decisions with major business, financial, or public-policy implications. Understanding your margin of error before presenting results protects you from overstating the confidence of your data and making costly decisions on uncertain findings.

Use this tool immediately after your survey closes to validate your results, or before you launch to set response targets that will give you the precision level you need. Quick answer

Margin of error in a survey measures how far your results might deviate from the true population value. It is calculated using the formula MOE = Z × √(p(1−p)/n), where Z is the confidence-level Z-score (1.96 for 95% confidence), p is the response proportion, and n is the sample size. A survey of 400 people typically yields a ±5% margin of error at 95% confidence. Use a free margin of error calculator to check the reliability of your results after any survey.

## Margin of Error Calculator FAQ

What is margin of error in a survey?

Margin of error is the range of values within which the true population value is likely to fall, given your sample size and confidence level. For example, a survey result of 60% with a ±5% margin of error means the true population value is likely between 55% and 65%. It quantifies the uncertainty inherent in any survey that doesn’t capture every person in the target population.
