Date, time and ranges, plus date of birth with age screening that stays correct next year.

Plus a prebuilt demographics bank, image hotspot and heatmap questions, and content blocks for stimulus.

Every type is authorable, persists, validates and analyses. Validation runs on the server when the answer is submitted, not only in the browser: required, minimum and maximum select, exclusive options, required Other text, matrix completeness, text length, word count, pattern and format, number bounds, slider ranges, constant sum totals, rank and match completeness, date bounds, and file count and type. A respondent cannot get past a rule by turning off JavaScript, so a dataset cannot quietly break the rules the questionnaire states.

Selection limits read as one plain rule on the kinds that take more than one answer: any number, at least, at most, exactly, or between. The respondent is told that rule above the options, before they start picking, rather than discovering that tiles have gone dead. Answers can be taken back, too. Re-picking a selected rating clears it, and an optional question a respondent clears still lets them continue, so nobody is stuck on a screen because of a mis-click on a question they never had to answer.

The questionnaire is written on a canvas that renders each question through the real respondent widget, on the survey's own live theme, and lets you edit it in place. Titles and descriptions are rich text with answer pipes and slash commands. Several authors can work in one survey at once, with character-level merge on the text, remote carets and collaborative undo, because a question's position is stored as a fractional order key rather than a slot in a list two people can move at the same time.

02Route it

## The part that decides _what happens next._

Thirteen logic tools decide who gets in, what they see and how they leave. The eight below do the heavy routing, and any respondent's exact path can be replayed.

![Respondent journey from entry through the screener and quota check to completion, with the screened out, quota full and dropped off counts branching off it](https://ik.imagekit.io/somanshu/onlinesurvey.ai/assets/landing-v2/J01-respondent-journey-graph.png?updatedAt=1788292215533&tr=orig-true)

Every path is explicit, and every respondent who leaves it carries the reason why.

Screeners

Qualify at the door, with a reason recorded.

Branching

If this, then that. First match wins.

Display logic

Show or hide a question, option or row.

Quotas

Cells reserve a slot, then close. Crossed cells too.

Carry forward

Selected, not selected, or all of them.

Loops and piping

Repeat a section per item, reuse the answers.

Scoring

Points on answers, banded at the end.

Endings

One per outcome, each with its own redirect.

Authors never write expressions. Every rule is if-then, assembled in one shared editor: pick a source, pick an operator, give it a value, and join clauses with AND or OR. Routing is forward only and fires when a respondent leaves a screen, first match wins, and there is exactly one ELSE, so two rules can never disagree about where somebody goes. A skipped screen never fires its own exit logic, which is the difference between a respondent who was not asked a question and one who answered it with nothing.

Visibility is a separate tool from routing. A condition can hide an element, an option or a single matrix row, and block entry gating suppresses a whole block including the screens further inside it. Carry-forward brings the options of a later question from an earlier answer, selected, unselected or all of them. Piping resolves an earlier answer into the wording of a later question and shows the option's label rather than its id. A block can be looped once per item from a driver list, with each iteration's answers kept apart.

Randomization is seeded rather than random, so a shuffled list is deterministic and anchored items stay put, and the order a given respondent saw can be replayed exactly. Quotas qualify at the door, cells reserve a slot and then close, and crossed cells work the same way. Endings are plural and you name them: as many as the study has outcomes, each tagged with the disposition it represents, completed, screened out, quota full or quality reject, and each with its own redirect. The status is read off the ending, so the outcome and the exit can never disagree.

Waves and versions

The questionnaire is frozen at publish, so wave four still matches wave one. Codes carry forward, changes are versioned.

Email campaigns are built end to end. Create a named campaign over a published survey, paste or import the recipients, and send now or on a schedule. Delivery, opens, clicks and unsubscribes are tracked for each recipient, a completed response is attributed back to the person who was invited, and an organization-wide suppression list is enforced so someone who opted out of one campaign is not invited to the next. Link and QR sharing carry a password, a scheduled open and close, and a response cap.

Publishing compiles an immutable, version-pinned snapshot of the questionnaire, and a respondent's session pins that version for its whole life. Somebody who started before an edit finishes the survey they started, not a half-changed one. That is what lets a tracker's later waves stack against its first instead of colliding with it, and it is why an option added mid-field never renumbers the codes already sitting in collected data. Authors keep editing the draft the entire time, and a content digest tells you whether an edit changes anything a respondent would actually see.

04Read it, then take it

## Six ways to read it. Four ways out.

One set of numbers behind every artefact, and the same numbers in the file you download.

Results

Live counts, percent of base and the right chart for every question.

Response grid

One row per respondent, searchable, with any path replayable.

Topline

Every question as counts and percent of base, with NETs, ready to send.

Document

An editable narrative written over the numbers.

Methodology

Generated from what actually happened in field.

Shared link

Frozen per link, with suppression and redaction always on.

Analytics reads the data live and precomputes nothing. Fieldwork shows the funnel, geography, device and browser, the questions people leave on, and completions over time. Results gives one card per question with the chart that question's type deserves and the raw numbers underneath it. The responses tab is a real spreadsheet: one column per question plus your score variables, session metadata, status pills, search, and cell selection you can copy straight into a sheet. Any single respondent's path can be replayed through the same engine that served it, screen by screen, with the reason each one was shown.

Percentages are of the base, meaning the distinct respondents who were actually asked, never of the sum of counts, and NET top and bottom two box rows come with the table rather than as a spreadsheet formula afterwards. Sharing is per link rather than per report: one report can be out at several addresses at once, each with its own expiry, its own audience and its own revocation, and each pinned by default to the numbers as they stood when that link was created. Rotating a link issues a new address carrying the same frozen numbers and retires the old one, so a client's copy never changes under them.

Your data out

Export controls, not just a download button.

SPSS .sav

Variable names, value labels, multiple response sets.

Excel

Labels or codes, loops wide or long.

CSV

Streaming, any size, never refused for being large.

Data dictionary

Every variable, its label and its provenance.

Every export is shaped by the request rather than by a fixed template: values as labels or as codes, multi-select as one column per choice or a single delimited column, loops wide or long, one header row or three, and filters for disposition, published version and date range. Labels are never truncated. A matrix, a rank or a constant sum contributes one column per row, slot or option instead of a cell holding a blob of JSON, and the variable names and answer codes are frozen at publish so two waves of the same tracker line up. Above a certain size an export hands back a background job instead of an error, and nothing is ever refused for being large.
