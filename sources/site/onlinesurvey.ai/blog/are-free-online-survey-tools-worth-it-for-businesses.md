## What "Free" Actually Means in Survey Software

Before comparing platforms, it helps to understand the three types of "free" in survey software:

**1\. Genuinely free**: no cost, no response cap, no feature expiry. Google Forms is the primary example. The trade-off is limited analytics and no AI features, but there are no hidden limits that surprise you mid-project.

**2\. Freemium with response caps**: free to use up to a set number of responses per survey or per month, then paid. Most platforms use this model. The free tier is usable but constrained: you hit the cap, the survey stops collecting, or responses are withheld until you upgrade. onlinesurvey.ai's Free plan sits in this category too, at 250 responses a month, which is high enough that most small projects never reach it.

**3\. Free trial**: full-featured access for a limited time period, then paid. Not genuinely free in the ongoing sense. Some platforms market these as "free plans" but they expire.

## Free vs. Paid: How to Decide

**Free survey software is the right choice when:**

- You're validating a research approach: before committing to a paid tool, use a free plan to confirm surveys will get the response rate and data quality you need
- Your volume is genuinely small: internal team surveys, occasional customer feedback, event polls that will never exceed the free plan's response cap
- You need basic data, not insights: you have an analyst who will interpret the data manually, so you don't need AI analysis or advanced analytics
- Cost is the only constraint: budget truly isn't available, and any research is better than none

**Paid survey software is worth the cost when:**

- You're running surveys regularly: monthly NPS, quarterly engagement, ongoing product research. The time saved by AI analysis compounds across every project.
- You hit the response cap: if a free plan's cap has stopped a survey mid-fieldwork, you've already lost more value than the cost of upgrading
- You need insights, not just counts: open-ended responses require NLP analysis to be useful at scale; free plans don't include it
- You need CRM integration: manual CSV export and import into Salesforce or HubSpot takes hours per survey; native integration eliminates that step
- GDPR compliance is required: free plans rarely include the full compliance tooling (consent management, data deletion workflows, EU data residency) that regulated business use requires
