Put a number next to an answer and the survey is scored. There is no switch to find: the points you typed are the scoring.

{/* screenshot: Scoring on a choice question: one row per answer, one number each. */}

## What it does

A survey has exactly one score, and every point you award lands in it. The **Scoring** dialog is where you award them, and what it shows depends on the question:

| How it scores | Questions | Rows come from |
|---|---|---|
| A number per answer | [Single choice](/questions/choice/single-choice), [Multiple choice](/questions/choice/multiple-choice), [Dropdown](/questions/choice/dropdown), the image choices, [Likert](/questions/ratings/likert), and the ready-made choice questions | The options you wrote |
| A number per scale point | [Star rating](/questions/ratings/star-rating), [Smiley rating](/questions/ratings/smiley-rating), [Thumbs up or down](/questions/ratings/thumbs-up-down), [NPS](/questions/ratings/nps), [Opinion scale](/questions/ratings/opinion-scale), [Yes or no](/questions/choice/yes-or-no) | The scale itself |
| A number per column | [Radio grid](/questions/grids/radio-grid), [Checkbox grid](/questions/grids/checkbox-grid), [Dropdown grid](/questions/grids/dropdown-grid), [Rating grid](/questions/grids/rating-grid) | The columns, applied to every row |
| Points per unit | [Numeric slider](/questions/ratings/numeric-slider) | One weight, multiplied by the value they chose |
| An answer key | Every text question | Accepted answers you type in the dialog |

Nothing is filled in for you, on any of them. What a 7 is worth is a decision about your study, not a property of the question, so every row starts blank. Blank is zero, negatives are allowed, and points are whole numbers.

On a grid, the columns are scored once and apply to every row. A row that is worded the other way round can take its own numbers with **Score this row differently**.

An answer key is compared without regard to capitals or spaces at the ends, so "Delivered On Time " matches "delivered on time".

The score is worked out afresh from all of the respondent's answers every time they submit a screen, so it is always in step with what they have said.

Questions whose answer is an order, an allocation, a date or a pin on an image cannot be scored. Their dialog says so rather than showing an empty table.

## Example

{/* screenshot: A scale generates its own rows: one per point. */}

In the logic showcase:

- "Which of these best describes you?" gives **Regular shopper** 5, **Occasional shopper** 2, **First visit** 1 and **Just browsing** -3
- "How likely are you to recommend us to a colleague?" gives **10** four points, **9** three, and **0** minus two
- "How much would you pay for this feature?" scores **1 point per unit** of the number they chose
- "What happened on this visit?" has an answer key: "delivered on time" is worth 3 and "late" is worth -1

The **Profile** block then opens only for respondents whose score is 5 or more.

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the dialog">
    Select the question and click **Scoring** in the panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type the numbers">
    Fill in the points for the answers that are worth something and leave the rest blank. On a long list, the **Filter** box narrows the rows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the score">
    The score is available as a condition source, a [branching](/logic/branching) source, a [quota](/logic/quotas) condition and an `@` [pipe](/logic/piping), under the name **score**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide whether respondents see it">
    In the rail, open **Survey** and switch **Show points to respondents** on to draw a points chip on scored questions. It is off unless you turn it on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Good to know

A question whose options are [carried forward](/logic/carry-forward) scores normally. The points belong to the source question's options, so they follow wherever those options land.

The score is a column in the Responses grid and in every [export](/results/exports).

Editing an answer after a response is in does not move the score. The number stays as the respondent earned it.

For a second number that is not the score, such as a count you build up yourself, use a number [variable](/logic/variables).
