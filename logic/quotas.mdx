A quota caps how many responses match a condition, 200 frequent shoppers say, and decides what happens to everyone who matches it after that.

{/* screenshot: The Quotas list: a name, a cap, who counts, and what happens when it is full. */}

## What it does

Each cell has four parts.

**Name** is what the cell is, in your words. It is what the fill meter and the reports call it.

**Cap** is the number of responses you want.

**Who counts** is a condition, built in the same editor every other rule uses. It can read any question in the survey, plus your [variables](/logic/variables) and the score.

**When full** is what happens to the next person who matches:

- **Send to an ending** finishes their survey on the ending you name, normally one tagged **Quota full**. See [endings](/survey/endings).
- **Let them finish, flagged** lets them complete the survey and marks the response.
- **Close the survey to new people** shuts the door for everyone. It is offered only where the cell's condition can be judged before anyone has answered anything, because that decision is made when a respondent arrives.

**Hold a slot** decides when a respondent starts occupying the cap. **On submit** counts finished responses only, so a cell can go slightly over: nobody who was inside while it was open is ever turned back. **From qualifying** holds a slot the moment they qualify and releases it after the number of minutes you set, which is exact but can turn away someone who would have made it, and can lose the slot for a slow respondent.

Someone turned away by a full cell lands on the ending you named, and their answers are kept. The response counts toward your plan allowance, but it does not fill the cell that turned them away and does not use up your survey's response cap. A respondent who is [screened out](/logic/screen-outs) releases any slot they were holding.

The refusal is a real ending, with your theme, your copy and your redirect.

## Example

{/* screenshot: One cell: the condition, the cap, the hold, and the ending it sends people to. */}

The logic showcase carries two:

- **Frequent shoppers**, cap 200. Who counts: "Do you shop with us more than once a month?" is **Yes, often**. It holds a slot from qualifying, for 30 minutes, and when it is full it sends people to the ending **We have enough answers**.
- **Just browsing**, cap 500. Who counts: "Which of these best describes you?" is **Just browsing**. When it is full, it lets them finish, flagged.

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Quotas">
    In the rail, open **Survey**, then click **Quotas** and **Add a quota**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the cell and set its cap">
    The name is what the meter reads, so "Women 25 to 34" beats "Quota 2".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say who counts">
    Build the condition under **Who counts**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose what happens when it fills">
    Set **When full**, and if you need the cap to be exact, switch **Hold a slot** to **from qualifying**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Good to know

A cell's fill is shown as a meter in the dialog, reading "48 of 200", and again on the survey's Overview.

Deleting a cell stops its cap applying. Responses you have already collected are not affected, and the dialog says so before you delete.

A cap on the survey as a whole, rather than on a group inside it, is not a quota. It is the response cap on the survey's settings.
