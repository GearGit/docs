Branching lets you send respondents down different paths through your survey depending on how they answer. This page explains how the rule editor works, what each piece of a rule means, and how to add your first branching rule.

## How Branching Rules Work

Every routing decision in OnlineSurvey is an **if-then** rule. You pick a question, choose a condition (such as "answer equals" or "answer contains"), and choose a destination. No expression language or formulas are involved. The rule editor guides you through each choice with dropdowns.

Rules within a set are evaluated in order, and the **first rule that matches wins**. Once a match is found, the remaining rules in that set are skipped. Each set of rules also has exactly one **Else** branch, which fires when none of the rules above it match.

<Note>
  Routing is forward-only. You can send a respondent ahead to a later question, block, or ending, but you cannot send them back to a question they have already seen.
</Note>

## Anatomy of a Rule

Each rule has three parts:

- **If:** The question you are evaluating.
- **Condition:** The comparison to apply, such as "is equal to," "is not equal to," "is any of," or "was answered."
- **Then go to:** The destination, which can be any question, block, or ending that appears later in the survey.

The **Else** row at the bottom of the rule set defines where respondents go when no rule matches. By default the Else destination is the next question in sequence.

## Adding a Branching Rule

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Logic panel">
    In the survey builder, select the question you want to branch from. Click the **Logic** tab in the right-hand panel.

    ![The Logic tab open on a question in the builder canvas](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a new rule">
    Click **Add rule**. A new if-then row appears with three empty fields.

    ![A blank if-then rule row with If, Condition, and Go To dropdowns](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose the condition">
    In the **If** dropdown, confirm the question you want to evaluate (it defaults to the current question). In the **Condition** dropdown, choose the comparison that fits your logic, for example "is equal to." In the value field that appears, enter or select the answer option the rule should match.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the destination">
    In the **Then go to** dropdown, choose where to send respondents who match this rule. You can pick any question, block, or ending that comes after this point in the survey.

    ![The Then go to dropdown open, showing a list of later questions and endings](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the Else destination">
    The **Else** row is already present at the bottom of the rule set. Click its destination dropdown and choose where respondents who do not match any rule should go. Leaving it on **Next question** is fine for most cases.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add more rules if needed">
    Click **Add rule** again to handle additional conditions. Rules are checked from top to bottom. Drag the handle on the left of any row to reorder rules.

    ![Multiple branching rules stacked in the Logic panel, with drag handles visible](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save and preview">
    Click **Save** in the builder toolbar, then open **Preview** to walk through the paths and confirm the routing behaves as expected.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tips for Clean Branching Logic

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Name your blocks" icon="tag">
    Giving blocks descriptive names in the builder makes the "Then go to" dropdown much easier to navigate, especially in longer surveys.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use endings as targets" icon="flag">
    You can route a respondent straight to a Thank You or Screened Out ending from any branching rule, not just from the last question.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Test every path" icon="flask">
    Use Preview mode and step through each answer option to verify that every branch lands where you expect.
  </Card>
  <Card title="First match wins" icon="bolt">
    Put your most specific conditions at the top of the rule list. A broad condition placed first will catch respondents before a narrower rule below it gets a chance to fire.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
