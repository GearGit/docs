## What it does

A pipe becomes a chip in the text. It holds a reference, not a copy, so the wording behind it stays live: rename the question and the chip re-reads, and the respondent always sees what they themselves answered.

You can pipe into a question title or description, the welcome screen, an ending, and an option label.

The menu offers three kinds of thing:

- **Earlier questions**, listed by their authored number: "Q2 Which of these have you used in the last month?". Only questions before this one appear, because a later answer does not exist yet.
- **Variables**, under their own heading, including the survey's score when any question awards points.
- **The loop value**, pinned at the top when the screen sits inside a [loop](/logic/loops), with a caption saying what it will be: "An answer picked on Q6", "An item from your list", or "1, 2, 3, one per pass".

What a respondent reads is the wording, never an internal reference. Piping a choice question shows the option label they picked, and piping a question they skipped shows nothing rather than a gap full of symbols. A pipe that points at a question you later deleted also renders as nothing.

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Put the caret where the answer should go">
    Click into the title, description, option label or ending copy on the canvas and type `@`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the source">
    Keep typing to narrow the list, by question number or by words in the title, then press Enter. A chip drops in where the caret was.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read it as a respondent">
    Open a [preview](/design/preview), answer the earlier question, and read the later screen back. Delete a chip like any other character.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Good to know

A pipe can only read a screen the respondent has already seen. That is the same rule every condition follows, and it is why the menu offers earlier questions only. See [how logic runs](/logic/how-logic-runs).

A question with several fields, such as [Address](/questions/ready-made/address), offers each field as its own row, numbered Q7.1, Q7.2 and so on.

Writing the answer into an ending is the usual way to end a scored survey with a result: pipe the score into the ending copy.
