The **Answer order** dialog decides what order a respondent sees a list in, and how much of it they see.

</Frame>

## What it does

**Order** is one setting with three values, because a list has one order:

- **As written**, the order you typed
- **Shuffled**, a different order for every respondent
- **Reversed**, the list the other way round for some respondents

Reversed is the treatment a scale wants. Shuffling "Strongly disagree" to "Strongly agree" destroys the measurement, so an ordered scale offers reverse and not shuffle, and the dialog says so: "A scale is measured by its order, so it is never shuffled. You can show it the other way round to some respondents."

**Show** chooses between **All of them** and **Only some**. Pick a number and each respondent sees that many, drawn from the list at random. The dialog does the arithmetic under the field: "Each respondent sees 3 of 5 choices".

**Pinning** holds an item in place whatever order is running. Pin to the top or to the bottom, one item at a time. "Other" and "None of these" are pinned to the bottom for you: "Other and None of these are never shuffled and always stay last."

A grid has two axes, and they behave differently. Its **rows** are the items, so they shuffle, reverse and subset. Its **columns** are the scale, so they only reverse.

Order is settled per respondent and stays that way. Someone who goes back a screen and returns sees the list exactly as they saw it the first time.

## Example

  <img src="/images/logic/randomization/matrix.png" alt="Answer order on a grid, with rows and columns" />

In the logic showcase:

- "Which of these best describes you?" is **shuffled**, with **Regular shopper** pinned first, and each respondent sees three of its four answers
- "The site is easy to use", a Likert question, is **reversed**
- "How did we do on each of these?" shuffles its rows, shows two of the three, and reverses its columns

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the dialog">
    Select the question and click **Answer order** in the panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the order">
    Choose **As written**, **Shuffled** or **Reversed**. On a grid, the rows and the columns have their own controls.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Shorten the list, if you want to">
    Switch **Show** to **Only some** and set the number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pin what must stay put">
    Filter the list and click **Pin** on any item that has to lead or trail.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Good to know

Shuffle, reverse and subset are offered on the lists where reordering means something: [Single choice](/questions/choice/single-choice), [Multiple choice](/questions/choice/multiple-choice), the image choices, the grids and the ready-made choice questions. A [Dropdown](/questions/choice/dropdown) shuffles and reverses but is never cut short, because it is usually a list somebody scans for an answer they already know. [Drag to rank](/questions/ranking/drag-to-rank), [Click to rank](/questions/ranking/click-to-rank) and [Constant sum](/questions/ranking/constant-sum) order but never subset: ranking a different set per respondent gives you answers that cannot be compared. [Likert](/questions/ratings/likert) reverses only.

The count is a ceiling. Carry-forward or a hidden option can leave fewer, and fewer is correct rather than a failure.

Showing only some options changes what a percentage is out of. Results divide each option by the people who were shown it.
