Every survey session ends with a **disposition**, a label that records how that session closed. The disposition tells you not just whether a respondent finished, but why the session ended the way it did. Each disposition maps to an **ending screen**, a final message the respondent sees before leaving the survey.

This page explains the four dispositions, how to configure ending screens, and why dispositions are important for data analysis.

## The Four Dispositions

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Complete" icon="circle-check">
    The respondent reached the end of the survey without being redirected or dropping off. These sessions form your core dataset.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Screened Out" icon="ban">
    A screen-out logic rule determined that the respondent did not qualify for the study. The session ends early and is recorded separately from completions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Quota Full" icon="chart-bar">
    The respondent qualified for the study, but the quota cell they belong to was already at its target count. The session closes without collecting further answers.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dropped Off" icon="person-walking-arrow-right">
    The respondent opened the survey and answered at least one question but did not reach an ending screen. Dropped-off sessions are incomplete but are still recorded, including all answers given before the respondent left.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Ending Screens

An ending screen is the final element a respondent sees when their session closes. It typically includes a short message and, optionally, a redirect URL that sends the respondent to another page after they read the message.

Every survey must have at least one ending screen. You can add multiple ending screens to give each disposition its own tailored message. For example:

- A **Complete** ending might thank the respondent and provide a confirmation code for an incentive.
- A **Screened Out** ending might thank the respondent for their time and explain that the study is not a match for them right now.
- A **Quota Full** ending might apologize that the study has reached capacity and invite the respondent to check back later.

![The ending screen editor showing a message field and an optional redirect URL field](/images/placeholder.png)

## Routing Sessions to the Right Ending

You connect a disposition to an ending screen through logic rules in the builder.

- **Screen-out rules** redirect a respondent to a specific screen-out ending when their answer fails a qualification check.
- **Quota-full rules** redirect a respondent to the quota-full ending when the relevant quota cell is closed.
- **The default flow** routes respondents who pass all checks and complete all questions to the completion ending.

<Tip>
If you are running a study through a panel provider, the redirect URL on each ending screen is where you paste the panel return links. Use the correct link for each disposition so the panel's system records the right outcome and issues the correct incentive.
</Tip>

## Why Dispositions Matter for Analysis

Dispositions let you filter and analyze your data with precision. When you export results or review them in the dashboard, you can separate completions from screen-outs and dropped-off sessions so that partial data does not distort your findings.

Dropped-off sessions are especially useful for diagnosing survey quality problems. A spike in drop-offs at a particular screen can indicate that a question is confusing, too sensitive, or that the survey is too long at that point.

<Note>
Quota-full sessions and screened-out sessions are not counted against your plan's response limit in the same way as completions. Check the [pricing page](https://onlinesurvey.ai/pricing) or contact support@onlinesurvey.ai if you have questions about how your plan counts each disposition type.
</Note>

<Warning>
A survey with no screen-out ending screen will display a blank or broken page if a screen-out rule fires and no ending has been assigned. Always create and assign a dedicated ending screen for every disposition you use in your logic.
</Warning>
