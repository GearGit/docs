Inline question editing lets you improve any question on your canvas by describing what you want in plain language. You do not need to edit the question yourself. Instead, you type an instruction, the AI proposes a revised version, and you decide whether to accept it, reject it, or refine it further. This page walks through the full editing flow and shares tips for getting the best results.

## How Inline Editing Works

The AI edit tool is attached to every question on the builder canvas. It reads the current question text, options, and settings, then applies your instruction to produce a revised version. The original question is never changed until you explicitly accept the proposal.

Instructions do not need to follow any special syntax or format. Write the way you would ask a colleague: "Make this shorter," "Add a Not Applicable option," or "Change the scale from 5 points to 7 points." The AI interprets plain language.

## Walking Through an Edit

<Steps>

  <Step title="Hover Over the Question You Want to Edit">
    On the builder canvas, move your cursor over the question card. A small toolbar appears along the top edge of the card, containing the edit, duplicate, and delete icons.

    ![A question card on the canvas with the hover toolbar visible at the top of the card](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click the AI Edit Icon">
    Click the **AI edit icon** (the sparkle or wand icon) in the question toolbar. A text input panel slides open beneath the question card.

    ![The AI edit panel open beneath a question card, showing an empty instruction field](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Type Your Instruction">
    In the instruction field, describe the change you want. You can be brief or detailed. Here are some examples of instructions that work well:

    - "Make this question shorter and more conversational."
    - "Add a Not Applicable option to the answer list."
    - "Split this into two separate questions."
    - "Rewrite this to be suitable for a B2B audience instead of consumers."
    - "Change the rating scale from 1-5 to 1-10 and update the labels."

    Type your instruction and click **Apply**.

    ![The instruction field with the example text 'Add a Not Applicable option' typed in, and the Apply button highlighted](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review the AI's Proposal">
    The AI displays a side-by-side view showing your original question on the left and the proposed revision on the right. Differences are highlighted so you can see exactly what changed.

    Take a moment to read the full proposal, including any answer options or logic the AI adjusted as part of the change.

    ![The side-by-side comparison view showing the original question on the left and the AI proposal on the right with changes highlighted](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Accept, Reject, or Refine">
    You have three options at this point:

    - **Accept** the proposal by clicking **Apply Change**. The canvas updates immediately and the original version is gone (though you can undo with Ctrl+Z or Cmd+Z).
    - **Reject** the proposal by clicking **Discard**. The question stays exactly as it was. The credit spent generating this proposal is not refunded, because the AI has already done the work.
    - **Refine** the proposal by typing a follow-up instruction in the same panel. For example, if the AI shortened the question but also removed an answer option you wanted to keep, type "Add back the 'Prefer not to say' option" and click **Apply** again.

    ![The proposal view with the Accept, Discard, and Refine instruction field visible at the bottom](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>

</Steps>

## Example: Editing a Satisfaction Question

Suppose you have the following question on your canvas:

> "On a scale from 1 to 5, how satisfied were you overall with the quality of the product you received in your most recent order?"

You want to shorten it and make it sound less formal. You open the AI edit panel and type:

> "Make this shorter and friendlier."

The AI might propose:

> "How satisfied were you with your most recent order? (1 = Not at all, 5 = Extremely)"

You review the change, decide the wording works, and click **Accept**. The updated question replaces the original on the canvas.

<Tip>
If the first proposal is close but not quite right, use a follow-up instruction rather than starting over. Follow-up instructions build on the current proposal, so you can dial in the exact phrasing in two or three short steps.
</Tip>

## What Inline Editing Can and Cannot Do

Inline editing works on a single question at a time. It can change question text, answer options, scale labels, and helper text. It cannot move a question to a different position in the survey or edit logic rules attached to other questions.

For changes that affect the whole survey structure, consider using [Generate from Brief](/ai/generate-from-brief) on a new draft, or make the structural changes manually in the builder.

<Note>
Inline editing reads only the question you select. If your question references earlier answers via piping or carry-forward, mention that context in your instruction so the AI can preserve it correctly.
</Note>

## Credit Usage

Each time you click **Apply** and the AI generates a proposal, one AI credit is spent. Clicking **Discard** after reviewing a proposal does not refund the credit, because the AI has already done the work. Clicking **Discard** before clicking **Apply** (that is, closing the panel without submitting an instruction) does not spend a credit.
