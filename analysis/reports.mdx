A report in OnlineSurvey is a document that wraps your survey numbers in a narrative you write and edit. This page covers creating a report from the Results view, adding charts, generating the methodology section, and sharing the finished document with stakeholders.

## What a Report Is

A report is a living document inside OnlineSurvey that combines editable prose with live question charts pulled from your survey data. Think of it as a research memo that always shows the current numbers without requiring you to export and paste charts manually.

When you are ready to share, you publish the report as a frozen shareable link. The link captures the state of the data at the moment of publication, so recipients always see a consistent snapshot even if more responses come in later.

## Creating a Report

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Results View">
    Go to your survey and click the **Results** tab. Make sure any filters you want to apply are set before you create the report, because the report inherits the active filter state as its default view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Create Report">
    Click **Create report** in the top-right corner of the Results view. OnlineSurvey opens a new document in the report builder and pre-populates it with your survey title, the collection date range, and a placeholder for the methodology section.

    ![The report builder open with a pre-populated title and blank narrative sections below](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write Your Narrative">
    Click anywhere in the document body to start typing. The editor works like a simple word processor: you can add headings, body text, and bullet lists. Each section of the report can hold a mix of prose paragraphs and embedded charts.

    Write your executive summary or key findings in this area. Keep each section focused on one theme so readers can navigate the report easily.

    ![The report builder with a paragraph of narrative text being typed in the editor](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Question Charts">
    To embed a chart from your survey, place your cursor where you want the chart to appear and click the **Insert chart** button in the editor toolbar. A picker lists every question in your survey. Select the question whose chart you want to embed, and the chart appears inline in the document.

    Embedded charts update automatically when the underlying data changes, as long as the report has not yet been published as a frozen link.

    ![The chart picker open in the report editor with a list of questions and one selected](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate the Methodology Section">
    Click **Generate methodology** in the document toolbar. OnlineSurvey reads the fieldwork metadata for your survey, including the number of responses, the collection dates, the published version, the distribution method, and any quota or screening criteria, and writes a methodology paragraph automatically.

    Review the generated text and edit it as needed. The generated methodology is a starting point, not a final draft.

    ![The methodology section of the report with auto-generated text describing fieldwork dates, sample size, and distribution method](/images/placeholder.png)
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Sharing a Report

When your report is ready to share, click **Share** in the top-right corner of the report builder. You will see the sharing configuration panel.

<Note>
  Every shareable link is a frozen snapshot. Recipients see the data and narrative as it stood when the link was generated. If you want to share an updated version later, generate a new link. Old links continue to show the older snapshot until they expire.
</Note>

Each link you generate has its own settings:

- **Expiry date:** set a date after which the link stops working. Leave blank for a link that never expires.
- **Audience label:** add an internal label (such as "Client review" or "Internal stakeholders") to help you track which link you shared with whom.

Suppression and redaction are always on for all shareable links. Open-ended verbatim responses that contain personally identifiable information flagged by the platform are suppressed automatically, and any fields you have marked as confidential in the survey setup are redacted from the shared view. You do not need to configure this manually.

<Tip>
  You can generate multiple links from the same report, each with different expiry dates and audience labels. Use separate links for your client and your internal team so you can expire the client link independently.
</Tip>

## Editing a Published Report

Generating a shareable link freezes the data snapshot for that link, but it does not lock the document itself. You can continue to edit the narrative, add charts, or update the methodology in the report builder at any time. Your edits are visible in the live builder but do not affect any frozen links already shared. To share the updated version, generate a new link.
