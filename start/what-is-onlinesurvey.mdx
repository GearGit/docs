OnlineSurvey is a platform for building research-grade surveys. You design the study, publish it, send it out, and read the results back in one place.

{/* screenshot: A survey open on the Build tab, with the lifecycle strip across the top. */}

## What it does

**Build.** The canvas shows the survey the way a respondent will see it, and you edit inside that rendering. There are 54 element types you can place, 49 of them questions and five of them content blocks such as a text block or an image banner.

**Add logic.** Branching, screen-outs, show and hide, carry-forward, piping, variables, scoring, randomization, quotas and loops. Every one of them is written as an if/then rule against an earlier answer. There is no expression language to learn.

**Share it.** Publish, then hand out a link, a QR code or an embed snippet, or send an email campaign to a recipient list.

**Read the results.** A spreadsheet of every session, per-question charts, a replay of any single respondent, reports you can share, and an AI pass that reads the data and narrates it.

Two kinds of people touch a survey. **You** are the author: you sign in, you belong to an organization and a workspace, and everything you do is checked against your role. A **respondent** is anonymous. They open a link, answer, and finish. They never have an account.

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a survey">
    From a workspace, click **New survey**, give it a title, and click **Create survey**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build it">
    Add questions on the canvas and write your logic. See [Build your first survey](/start/build-your-first-survey).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish it">
    Publishing freezes a numbered version and opens your link. See [Publish and share](/start/publish-and-share).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read what comes back">
    Responses arrive as they are collected. See [Read your first responses](/start/read-your-first-responses).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Good to know

**Preview is the real thing.** The builder's preview runs the same engine a respondent runs, so routing, visibility and piping behave exactly as they will in the field. It creates no session and records nothing.

**Publishing is never blocked by how you built your survey.** An empty option, a rule that can never match, a screen nobody reaches: all of those publish. Testing the study before it goes live is yours to do, and preview and test responses are there for it.

**A published version is frozen.** Respondents read the version they started on for the whole of their run, while you keep editing the draft.
