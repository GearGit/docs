A video block puts one clip on a screen and asks for nothing back. Use it for an advertisement respondents are about to react to, a demonstration of the thing you are asking about, or a short welcome in your own voice.

{/* screenshot: A video block as a respondent sees it, playing in place above the questions. */}

## What it is

One video in a box you size. It plays on the screen, where it sits: nobody leaves the survey to watch it, and nobody opens another site. Like an [image banner](/questions/content/image-banner) it carries no caption of its own, so words about the clip go in a [text block](/questions/content/text-block) above or below it. It takes no answer, so it has no **Required** toggle, no **Validation** row and no logic of its own.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Video">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Video**, in the **Content** group. It arrives as an empty box.

    {/* screenshot: An empty video block on the builder canvas, showing the Add video slot. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the video">
    Click **Add video** on the box. The picker opens on three tabs: **Library**, for a file your organization has already uploaded, **Upload**, for a new one, and **Link**, which takes a YouTube or a Vimeo address as well as a direct link to a file. Uploading is on a paid plan. A link works on every plan.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the block on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Aspect ratio** | The shape of the box: **16:9**, **4:3** or **1:1 (Square)**. |
| **Autoplay (muted)** | The clip starts on its own, with the sound off, when the screen opens. |
| **Loop** | The clip starts again when it reaches the end. |
| **Show controls** | Gives the respondent the play bar, so they can pause, seek and set the volume. On by default. |

The three playback settings apply to a video file, whether you uploaded it or linked straight to it. A YouTube or Vimeo address plays in that service's own player instead, which brings its own controls with it.
