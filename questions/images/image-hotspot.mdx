Image hotspot puts one picture in front of a respondent and asks them to mark the part of it that answers the question. Every tap drops a numbered pin where they touched, so the answer is a position on the image rather than a word. Use it when you want to know where somebody looked, clicked or objected, and naming the places in advance would give the answer away.

{/* screenshot: An image hotspot question as a respondent sees it, with numbered pins on the picture. */}

## When to use it

- You are testing a design, a pack or a page and want the spot that drew attention, or the spot that lost it.
- The parts have no agreed names: a diagram, a map, a shelf, a room, a garment.
- Naming the options in a list would prompt the answer, and you want the respondent to find it themselves.

**Consider instead:** [Image single select](/questions/choice/image-single-select) when whole pictures are the options and one wins, [Image multi select](/questions/choice/image-multi-select) when several can, [Single choice](/questions/choice/single-choice) when the parts can be named in words and you want them counted cleanly.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Image Hotspot">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Image Hotspot**, under the **Media** heading. The question lands where the gap was with an empty picture area that reads **Add image**.

    {/* screenshot: An image hotspot question on the builder canvas, with the empty image area. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the picture and write the question">
    Click **Add image** on the canvas, or the **Image** row in the panel, and the media picker opens on three tabs: **Library** for what your organization already has, **Upload** for a new file, **Link** for a file that lives elsewhere. Uploads are available on a paid plan. Then type the question and its description straight onto the canvas.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Image** | The picture respondents mark. Opens the same media picker the canvas does, and lets you **Replace** or **Remove** what is there. |
| **Aspect ratio** | The shape the picture is drawn at: **16:9**, **16:10**, **4:3** or **1:1 (Square)**. It frames the picture on the screen, and the heatmap in your results draws the pins over the picture at the same shape. |

Below those the panel holds **Required**, **Validation**, and the three logic rows every question has: **Show/hide question**, **Skip from here** and **Set a variable**.

{/* screenshot: The question panel for an image hotspot, with the image row and the aspect ratio row. */}

## Rules you can set

**Required** is a toggle in the panel. The pin counts are behind **Validation**, one row below it, under the caption **pins**.

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they place at least one pin. |
| [Min pins](/rules/number-bounds) | The fewest marks you accept. Short of it, the answer reads "Place at least 3 pins." |
| [Max pins](/rules/number-bounds) | The most you accept. Until you set it, the picture takes three. |
| [Custom error](/rules/custom-messages) | One sentence that replaces the standard wording when any rule on this question stops them. |

The picture carries the count as they work, so nobody has to guess: it reads TAP TO MARK with how many more are needed while a minimum is unmet, then how many are left, then MAX PINS REACHED once the cap is reached and further taps do nothing. A **Clear pins** button appears under the picture as soon as there is a pin, and it clears all of them, so a respondent who mismarks starts again rather than hunting for the pin to undo. There is also a control to open the picture full screen, which is where fine detail gets marked accurately, and pins can be placed there as well.

## Logic that works with it

The answer is a set of points on a picture. Nothing in it is a word or a number, so conditions read whether it was answered and nothing more: **is answered** and **is not answered**. That is still enough to show a follow-up question to the people who marked something, or to skip ahead for the people who did not. It cannot be a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source or target, because it holds no list of answers to hand on, and it cannot [drive a loop](/logic/loops) for the same reason. It does not [score](/logic/scoring): points are typed next to an answer you wrote, and a position on an image is not one, so this question has no **Scoring** row. Where a pin lands is a result to read rather than a [condition](/logic/how-logic-runs) to branch on, so plan to analyze it rather than route on it.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Heatmap**, which draws every respondent's pins as dots over the same picture, so the crowded places stand out where the dots pile up. Clicking one dot picks out that respondent's pins and fades the rest, a caption counts the pins and the responses they came from, and the picture opens full screen for a closer look. **Values list** is the other entry in **Chart type**.

In every export the question becomes one column, holding the position of every pin that respondent placed.
