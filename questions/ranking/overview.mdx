These four questions ask about a list as a whole rather than about one item at a time. Ranking is the right tool when what you want is relative order: which of these matters most, and after that, and after that. A rating gives you an absolute score for each item and lets a respondent call all five of them important, which is exactly what happens when everything on the list sounds good. [Drag to rank](/questions/ranking/drag-to-rank) and [Click to rank](/questions/ranking/click-to-rank) are the same question with two gestures, and they force the comparison a scale lets people avoid. [Constant sum](/questions/ranking/constant-sum) is the one to reach for when the trade-off is the point: a fixed budget of points, percent or money, split across the items, so saying more about one means saying less about another. [Match pairs](/questions/ranking/match-pairs) is a different job again, pairing each item on the left with one on the right.

## Compare

| | Answers with | Can be required | Drives a loop | Scorable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Drag to rank](/questions/ranking/drag-to-rank) | The options in the order they put them | Yes | Yes | No |
| [Click to rank](/questions/ranking/click-to-rank) | The options in the order they clicked them | Yes | Yes | No |
| [Constant sum](/questions/ranking/constant-sum) | An amount per option, adding up to the total | Yes | No | No |
| [Match pairs](/questions/ranking/match-pairs) | One right-hand item per left-hand item | Yes | No | No |

## Why none of them scores

An order and an allocation both refuse points, and for the same reason: there is nothing to put a number against that is not already the answer.

Every respondent ranks every item, so a number beside each option awards the identical total to everyone and the ranking itself changes nothing. The honest alternatives are points for a position or an answer key, and neither is a column of numbers beside the options. A constant sum is already a number per option, so points could only multiply a share or test a threshold, and a threshold is a condition. Put one in a named variable with **Set a variable** instead. See [Scoring](/logic/scoring) for the kinds that do take points.

Both ranking questions are [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) sources and both [drive a loop](/logic/loops), so the items somebody ranked can become a later question's list or a block that repeats once per item. A constant sum takes a carried list into its own options but is neither a source nor a driver, and match pairs is neither.
