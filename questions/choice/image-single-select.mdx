Image single select lays the options out as picture tiles and takes one of them. Use it when the thing being chosen is easier to recognize than to describe: a pack design, a layout, a logo, a color.

{/* screenshot: An image single select question as a respondent sees it, a grid of picture tiles with one selected. */}

## When to use it

- You are testing creative work, and what people see is the question.
- The options are visual and naming them would prime the answer: "the blue one" is already a judgment.
- Your respondents are choosing between things they recognize on sight, such as brands or packaging.

**Consider instead:** [Image multi select](/questions/choice/image-multi-select) when more than one picture can be chosen, [Single choice](/questions/choice/single-choice) when words describe the options perfectly well and load faster, [Image hotspot](/questions/images/image-hotspot) when the answer is a place on one picture rather than a choice between several, [Drag to rank](/questions/ranking/drag-to-rank) when the order they would put them in is the answer.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Image Single Select">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Image Single Select**, under the **Choice** heading. The question lands where the gap was, with one empty tile whose picture slot reads **Add image**.

    {/* screenshot: The image single select question on the builder canvas, one tile filled and one empty. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question and fill the tiles">
    Type the question straight onto the canvas. Click **Add image** on a tile and the media picker opens on three tabs: **Library**, what your organization has already uploaded; **Upload**, which adds a new file on a paid plan; and **Link**, which takes the address of a picture already on the web. Confirm with **Use image**. The caption under the tile is typed in place, **Add tile** appends another, and **Crop this picture** sets **How the picture fills its tile**, **Cover** or **Contain**, and the **Focus point** that must stay in frame.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Options | The tiles they pick from, each one a picture and a caption. Edited on the canvas, or in **Bulk edit** as plain text, one caption per line. A line you leave alone keeps its image. |
| **Columns** | How many tiles sit in a row: **2**, **3** or **4**. Fewer columns means bigger pictures. |
| **Aspect ratio** | The shape every tile is drawn at: **4:3**, **1:1 (Square)** or **16:9**. Pictures of different shapes are cropped to it, which is what **Crop this picture** adjusts. |
| **Show labels** | Whether the caption shows under each tile. Turn it off when the picture is the whole question and a word under it would lead the answer. |
| **"Other" with a text box** | Adds an **Other** option with a text box beside it. It always shows last. |
| **"None of these" option** | Adds an option that stands for none of the rest. It always shows last. |
| **Answer order** | **As written**, **Shuffled** or **Reversed**. Shuffle is worth reaching for here, because the tile in the top left is picked more often than it deserves. |
| **How many each respondent sees** | Inside the **Answer order** dialog. **All of them**, or **Only some**, which draws that many tiles for each respondent. |

{/* screenshot: The question panel for an image single select, with the columns, aspect ratio and show labels rows. */}

## Rules you can set

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they pick a tile. If they pick **Other**, the box beside it has to be filled in too. |

Required is the whole of it. One tile is either chosen or it is not, so this question has no **Validation** row in the panel.

## Logic that works with it

The answer is the one tile they picked, stored as the option itself rather than as its caption or its picture, so changing either one later never moves a rule that points at it. Conditions read it with **is**, **is not**, **is one of**, **is not one of**, **is answered** and **is not answered**, and you pick the tile from a list of the captions you wrote. It is a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source, so a later question can offer the tile they chose, the ones they left, or all of them, and it takes a carried list into its own tiles in the same way. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), repeating a block once for the tile they picked, once per tile they left, or once per tile in the set. It [scores](/logic/scoring) points per option. [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the caption, so give a tile a caption even when you hide it.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Bar chart**, one bar per tile, counting the respondents who picked it. **Pie chart** and **Values list** are the other two entries in **Chart type**. The chart reads by caption, which is the other reason to write one.

In every export the question becomes one coded column: a number for the tile they picked, with its caption attached.
