Occupation is a text box for a job, in the respondent's own words, so nobody has to find themselves in a list of categories you wrote. You get free text, charted as a word cloud so the common jobs stand out before you have coded anything.

{/* screenshot: An Occupation question as a respondent sees it, one box with Enter occupation written in it. */}

## When to use it

- You want what people actually call their job, not the nearest thing on a list of categories.
- You are profiling a sample and job titles vary too widely to write out.
- You plan to read the answers and group them yourself afterward.

**Consider instead:** [Short text](/questions/text/short-text) when the answer is not a job, or when you want to set the hint inside the box yourself, [Single choice](/questions/choice/single-choice) when you have a fixed list of roles and want everyone in one of them, [Education level](/questions/ready-made/education-level) for the other standard profiling question with a written list behind it.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Occupation">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Occupation**. It arrives as one box with "Enter occupation…" as the hint inside it, and no question wording of its own.

    {/* screenshot: An Occupation question on the builder canvas, its wording typed in place. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question">
    The canvas is the real survey, so you type the question and its description straight onto it. A description such as "Your job title, or what you spend most of your working day on" is what keeps the answers comparable.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

An Occupation question has nothing to configure. The panel shows **Type**, fixed at **Occupation**, then **Required** and **Validation**, then the logic rows. The hint inside the box comes with the question type and stays as it is; the description on the canvas is where you say what counts as an answer, because the box takes whatever they type.

{/* screenshot: The question panel for an Occupation question, with Type fixed and Required under it. */}

## Rules you can set

**Required** is a toggle in the panel. Everything else is behind **Validation**, one row below it.

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until the box has something in it. |
| [Text limits](/rules/text-limits) | **Min characters**, **Max characters**, **Min words** and **Max words**. A maximum of a few words keeps a job title from turning into a paragraph. |
| [Pattern](/rules/patterns) | Accepts the answer only when it matches a shape you pick, such as **Letters only**. |
| [Custom error](/rules/custom-messages) | One sentence that replaces the standard wording when any rule on this question stops them. |

## Logic that works with it

The answer is the text they typed, so conditions read it as text: **is**, **is not**, **contains**, **does not contain**, **starts with**, **ends with**, **is answered** and **is not answered**. **contains** is the one that earns its keep, since "nurse" catches a staff nurse and a nurse practitioner alike. It is not a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source, and nothing carries into it, because there is no list of choices to pass along. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), which gives one pass for the job they named. It [scores](/logic/scoring) against an answer key, the accepted answers you type in the **Scoring** dialog, compared without regard to capitals or spaces at the ends. [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the job back to them in the words they used.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Word cloud**, sized by how often a word comes up across the answers. **Values list** and **Bar chart** are the other two entries in **Chart type**, and the list is where you go to read the answers one by one.

In every export the question becomes one text column, holding the answer as it was typed.

Occupation is not a contact detail, so unlike [Name](/questions/ready-made/name) or [Email address](/questions/ready-made/email-address) it appears on a shared [report](/results/reports) like any other question.
