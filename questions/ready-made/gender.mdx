Gender is a single choice question that arrives with its answers already written. Respondents pick one of five, including an option for people who would rather describe themselves and one for people who would rather not say. Every word of it is yours to change on the canvas.

{/* screenshot: A Gender question as a respondent sees it, five options with one to pick. */}

## When to use it

- You are profiling a sample and want the standard wording rather than a list you drafted under time pressure.
- You want to cross-tabulate the rest of the study by gender in your results.
- You are screening or filling [quotas](/logic/quotas) on a gender balance.

**Consider instead:** [Single choice](/questions/choice/single-choice) when the list is not about gender, [Age group](/questions/ready-made/age-group) and [Marital status](/questions/ready-made/marital-status) for the other profiling questions that ship with their answers written, [Short text](/questions/text/short-text) when you want it in the respondent's own words and no list at all.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Gender">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Gender**. It arrives with five options written: Female, Male, Non-binary, Prefer to self-describe, Prefer not to say.

    {/* screenshot: A Gender question on the builder canvas, its five options edited in place. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question and edit the options">
    Type the question on the canvas. The options are ordinary options: reword one in place, click **Add option** at the end of the list to add another, or open **Bulk edit** from the chip beside it and paste a list, one per line.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Options | The five they pick from. Edited on the canvas, or in **Bulk edit** as plain text, one per line. |
| **"Other" with a text box** | Adds an **Other** option with a text box beside it, which is the alternative to the self-describe option that ships. It always shows last. |
| **"None of these" option** | Adds an option that stands for none of the rest. Picking it clears anything else they had picked. It always shows last. |
| **Answer order** | **As written**, **Shuffled** or **Reversed**. Shuffled draws a fresh order for each respondent; reversed turns the list around for about half of them. The same person sees the same order every time they come back to the screen. |
| **How many each respondent sees** | Inside the **Answer order** dialog. **All of them**, or **Only some**, which draws that many options for each respondent. Pinned options, **Other** and **None of these** are outside the draw and show to everyone. |

{/* screenshot: The question panel for a Gender question, with the option list settings and the answer order row. */}

## Rules you can set

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they pick an option. |

Required is the whole of it. One option is either chosen or it is not, so a Gender question has no **Validation** row in the panel and nothing else to constrain.

## Logic that works with it

The answer is one option, stored as the option itself rather than as its wording, so rewording an option never moves a rule that points at it. Conditions read it with **is**, **is not**, **is one of**, **is not one of**, **is answered** and **is not answered**, and you pick the value from the list you wrote. It is a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source, so a later question can offer the option they chose, the ones they left, or all of them, and a carried list can land in its options in the same way. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), over the option they chose, the ones they left, or the whole list. It [scores](/logic/scoring) points per option, and a point can be negative. Individual options can be shown or hidden by a rule with [Show/hide option](/logic/show-and-hide). [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the option in the wording they saw.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Bar chart**, one bar per option, counting the respondents who picked it. **Pie chart** and **Values list** are the other two entries in **Chart type**.

In every export the question becomes one coded column, holding a code for the option they picked with your wording attached to it. Each answer keeps the wording it was shown, so rewording an option later never rewrites the answers already given.
