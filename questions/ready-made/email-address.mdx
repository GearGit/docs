Email address collects one address and checks it when the respondent leaves the box. A tick and "Looks good." appear once the address parses, and a slip in a common domain draws a "Did you mean" line under it, with the fix one tap away. An address that does not parse is refused when they try to move on.

{/* screenshot: An Email address question as a respondent sees it, the tick showing once the address parses. */}

## When to use it

- You are collecting an address for a follow-up, a report back, or entry into a draw.
- You want the obvious typos caught before the answer is stored rather than after.
- You want the answer held back from any report you share, without remembering to do anything about it.

**Consider instead:** [Email](/questions/text/email) when you want to set the hint inside the box or switch the question to another text type later; it checks the address and is held back from shared reports in the same way. [Mobile phone](/questions/ready-made/mobile-phone) when a call or a text suits better, [Name](/questions/ready-made/name) when you want who they are as well.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Email Address">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Email Address**. It arrives as one box with "your@email.com" as the hint inside it, and no question wording of its own.

    {/* screenshot: An Email address question on the builder canvas, its wording typed in place. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question">
    The canvas is the real survey, so you type the question and its description straight onto it. The hint and the checking come with the question type and stay as they are.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

An Email address question has nothing to configure. The panel shows **Type**, fixed at **Email Address**, then **Required** and **Validation**, then the logic rows. The typo suggestion is a nudge and never a refusal: a respondent who means what they typed carries on. What does stop them is an address that does not parse, with "Enter a valid email address."

{/* screenshot: The question panel for an Email address question, with Type fixed and Required under it. */}

## Rules you can set

**Required** is a toggle in the panel. Everything else is behind **Validation**, one row below it.

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until the box has an address in it. |
| [Text limits](/rules/text-limits) | **Min characters**, **Max characters**, **Min words** and **Max words**. |
| [Pattern](/rules/patterns) | Narrows what is accepted beyond the format check the question already makes, which is how you take addresses at your own domain and nothing else. |
| [Custom error](/rules/custom-messages) | One sentence that replaces the standard wording when any rule on this question stops them. |

## Logic that works with it

The answer is the address as text, so conditions read it as text: **is**, **is not**, **contains**, **does not contain**, **starts with**, **ends with**, **is answered** and **is not answered**. The domain is the end of the answer, so **ends with** is how you route everyone from one company down their own path. It is not a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source, and nothing carries into it, because there is no list of choices to pass along. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), which gives one pass for the address they gave. It [scores](/logic/scoring) against an answer key, the accepted answers you type in the **Scoring** dialog. [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the address back to them, which is how a last screen confirms where the results will go.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Values list**, the answers themselves with a count beside each. **Word cloud** is the other entry in **Chart type**. An address is unique per respondent, which is why a list of values is the useful view and a distribution is not.

In every export the question becomes one text column, holding the address as it was typed.

Email addresses never appear on a shared [report](/results/reports). Along with names, phone numbers and postal addresses they are held back on every link, with no setting to change.
