Education level is a single choice question that arrives with seven levels already written, from Less than high school to Doctorate, plus an option for people who would rather not say. Respondents pick the highest one they finished.

{/* screenshot: An Education level question as a respondent sees it, seven levels with one to pick. */}

## When to use it

- You are profiling a sample and want education as one of the standard breaks in your results.
- You are screening for a study that needs a particular level of education.
- You want a list that runs in order, so the results read as a progression rather than as unrelated categories.

**Consider instead:** [Single choice](/questions/choice/single-choice) when the levels in your country do not match the ones that ship and you would rather start from nothing, [Occupation](/questions/ready-made/occupation) when what someone does matters more than what they studied, [Age group](/questions/ready-made/age-group) and [Marital status](/questions/ready-made/marital-status) for the other profiling questions that come with their answers written.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Education Level">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Education Level**. It arrives with seven levels written: Less than high school, High school, Some college, Bachelor’s degree, Master’s degree, Doctorate, Prefer not to say.

    {/* screenshot: An Education level question on the builder canvas, its seven levels edited in place. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question and edit the levels">
    Type the question on the canvas. The levels are ordinary options: reword one in place, click **Add option** at the end of the list to add another, or open **Bulk edit** from the chip beside it and paste the levels your country uses, one per line.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Options | The levels they pick from. Edited on the canvas, or in **Bulk edit** as plain text, one per line. Write them from lowest to highest, because that is the order a reader expects. |
| **"Other" with a text box** | Adds an **Other** option with a text box beside it, so somebody can name a qualification your list does not cover. It always shows last. |
| **"None of these" option** | Adds an option that stands for none of the rest. Picking it clears anything else they had picked. It always shows last. |
| **Answer order** | **As written**, **Shuffled** or **Reversed**. The levels run in a sequence, so **As written** is the usual choice here. |
| **How many each respondent sees** | Inside the **Answer order** dialog. **All of them**, or **Only some**, which draws that many options for each respondent. |

{/* screenshot: The question panel for an Education level question, with the option list settings and the answer order row. */}

## Rules you can set

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they pick a level. |

Required is the whole of it. One level is either chosen or it is not, so an Education level question has no **Validation** row in the panel and nothing else to constrain.

## Logic that works with it

The answer is one level, stored as the option itself rather than as its wording, so rewording a level never moves a rule that points at it. Conditions read it with **is**, **is not**, **is one of**, **is not one of**, **is answered** and **is not answered**, and you pick the level from the list you wrote. **is one of** is how you group several levels into one branch, such as any degree at all. It is a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source, and a carried list can land in its options. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), over the level they chose, the ones they left, or the whole list. It [scores](/logic/scoring) points per option, and a point can be negative. Individual levels can be shown or hidden by a rule with [Show/hide option](/logic/show-and-hide). [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the level in the wording they saw.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Bar chart**, one bar per level, counting the respondents who picked it. **Pie chart** and **Values list** are the other two entries in **Chart type**.

In every export the question becomes one coded column, holding a code for the level they picked with your wording attached to it. Each answer keeps the wording it was shown, so rewording a level later never rewrites the answers already given.
