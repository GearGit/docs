Opinion scale puts eleven numbered buttons in front of a respondent, 0 to 10, with a caption at each end that you write. It is the same instrument as [NPS](/questions/ratings/nps) without the recommendation framing, so use it whenever you want a wide numbered scale for something else entirely.

{/* screenshot: An opinion scale question as a respondent sees it, eleven numbered buttons between two captions. */}

## When to use it

- You want a wide scale for a judgment that is not about recommending: value for money, ease, confidence, likelihood of returning.
- You want the answer as a number you can average, threshold and branch on, rather than as a named point.
- You are writing both ends yourself and the standard recommendation wording would be wrong.

**Consider instead:** [NPS](/questions/ratings/nps) when the question really is about recommending and you want the score reported as one, [Star rating](/questions/ratings/star-rating) when a shorter 3, 5, 7 or 10 point scale reads better, [Likert](/questions/ratings/likert) when the points are words rather than numbers, [Numeric slider](/questions/ratings/numeric-slider) when the range is wider than a row of buttons can hold.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Opinion Scale">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Opinion Scale**, under **Rating**. The question lands where the gap was with the full 0 to 10 scale drawn and both end captions filled in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question and the two ends">
    Type the question on the canvas, then write the two captions in the panel so the ends say what your scale measures.

    {/* screenshot: The opinion scale question on the builder canvas, edited in place. */}
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **High label** | The caption under the right end of the scale. Until you write one it reads **Extremely likely**, which is what respondents see. |
| **Low label** | The caption under the left end. Until you write one it reads **Not at all likely**. |

The wording it starts with is the recommendation question's, and it stays until you write your own, so the two captions are part of setting the question up.

The scale is fixed at eleven points, 0 to 10, and there is no control that changes it. The **Type** row at the top of the panel switches this question to [NPS](/questions/ratings/nps) and back, which is the same scale under the name your reporting uses.

{/* screenshot: The question panel for an opinion scale, with the high and low label rows. */}

## Rules you can set

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they pick a number. |

Required is the whole of it. Every answer the scale offers is already inside its range, so there is nothing left to bound and the panel has no **Validation** row.

## Logic that works with it

The answer is a number, so conditions compare it as one: **is**, **is not**, **is more than**, **is at least**, **is less than**, **is at most**, **is between**, **is answered** and **is not answered**. It [scores](/logic/scoring) points per scale point, one row per point from 0 to 10, and a point can be negative. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), either one pass for the answer they gave or as many passes as the number they chose. It is not a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source and nothing carries into it, because a rating is a measurement rather than a list of things worth asking about again. [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the number they picked.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Bar chart**, one bar per point from 0 to 10, counting the respondents who picked it. **Histogram** and **Pie chart** are the other two entries in **Chart type**.

In every export the question becomes one column holding the number that respondent picked.
