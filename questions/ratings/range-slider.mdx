Range slider is a track with two handles on it, and the answer is the band between them. A respondent drags one handle to their low figure and the other to their high one, and both numbers are shown once the band is set.

{/* screenshot: A range slider question as a respondent sees it, two handles with the band between them filled in. */}

## When to use it

- The honest answer is a range rather than a figure: what they would pay, how long a job takes, how many hours a week.
- You want to know how wide their band is, not only where it sits.
- A single number would force a false precision that makes the data worse.

**Consider instead:** [Numeric slider](/questions/ratings/numeric-slider) when one number is the answer and you want to average it, score it or compare it, [Text slider](/questions/ratings/text-slider) when the stops should be words, [Number](/questions/text/number) when they know the figure and would rather type it, [Date range](/questions/dates/date-range) when the band is a span of days.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Slider Range">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Slider Range**, under **Rating**. It arrives as a track from 0 to 100, moving in steps of 1, with both end numbers drawn.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question and set the track">
    Type the question on the canvas, set the step and the units in the panel, and set the two ends of the track under **Validation**.

    {/* screenshot: The range slider question on the builder canvas, edited in place. */}
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Step** | How far a handle moves at a time. It arrives on 1, so a track from 0 to 100 offers a hundred stops. Raise it to 5 or 10 for a coarser band. |
| **Show min/max labels** | Draws the two end numbers at either side of the track. It starts on. |
| **Unit before** | Text printed in front of a number, such as a currency symbol. It captions both handles. |
| **Unit after** | Text printed after a number, such as a weight or a percent sign. |

Units are a caption on the numbers. The answer stored is the two bare figures, so nothing in your charts or exports has to know they are there.

{/* screenshot: The question panel for a range slider, with the step, labels and unit rows. */}

## Rules you can set

**Required** is a toggle in the panel. The two ends of the track are behind **Validation**, one row below it.

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they set the band. A slider nobody has touched draws two hollow handles on an empty track rather than a full bar, so an untouched question never looks answered. |
| [Minimum and Maximum](/rules/number-bounds) | The two ends of the track, which are also the bound. A respondent cannot drag past them, and a value outside them is refused. |
| [Custom error](/rules/custom-messages) | One sentence that replaces the standard wording when a rule on this question stops them. |

{/* screenshot: Minimum and Maximum in the Validation dialog, the two ends of the track. */}

## Logic that works with it

The answer is two numbers, a low and a high, rather than one value, so there is nothing for a comparison to put on one side of it. Conditions can ask whether the band was set at all, **is answered** and **is not answered**, and that is the whole list. For a rule that turns on how much somebody would pay, ask for the figure with a [Numeric slider](/questions/ratings/numeric-slider) or a [Number](/questions/text/number) and compare that.

For the same reason it does not [score](/logic/scoring), so the panel has no **Scoring** row: there is no single value to weight. It cannot [drive a loop](/logic/loops), and the loop dialog leaves it out of the list of drivers. It is not a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source and nothing carries into it. [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the band they chose, the low and the high together.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Bar chart**. **Histogram** and **Pie chart** are the other two entries in **Chart type**, and **Raw data** under the card lists the bands one respondent at a time.

In every export the question becomes one column.
