NPS is the standard recommendation question: eleven numbered buttons from 0 to 10, with a caption at each end. The range is fixed, which is the point of it, because a score only means something next to other scores collected the same way.

{/* screenshot: An NPS question as a respondent sees it, eleven buttons from 0 to 10 with a caption at each end. */}

## When to use it

- You are tracking recommendation over time and want a number your last wave can be compared against.
- You want one headline figure for a whole study, reported the way the rest of your field reports it.
- You want to follow the rating with a reason, using the number they gave to decide which question to ask next.

**Consider instead:** [Opinion scale](/questions/ratings/opinion-scale) for the same 0 to 10 scale without the recommendation framing, [Star rating](/questions/ratings/star-rating) when you want 3, 5, 7 or 10 points instead, [Likert](/questions/ratings/likert) when the answer is agreement with a statement, [Smiley rating](/questions/ratings/smiley-rating) when you are after a feeling rather than a figure.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick NPS">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **NPS**, under **Rating**. The question lands where the gap was with the full 0 to 10 scale drawn and both end captions already reading **Not at all likely** and **Extremely likely**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question">
    Type the question on the canvas. The wording is yours, so ask about whatever you are measuring recommendation of.

    {/* screenshot: The NPS question on the builder canvas, edited in place. */}
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **High label** | The caption under the right end of the scale. Left alone it reads **Extremely likely**, which is what respondents see. |
| **Low label** | The caption under the left end. Left alone it reads **Not at all likely**. |

That is the whole panel for display. The scale itself is fixed at eleven points, 0 to 10, and there is no control that changes it. If you want a different length, use [Star rating](/questions/ratings/star-rating), [Smiley rating](/questions/ratings/smiley-rating) or [Likert](/questions/ratings/likert) instead.

{/* screenshot: The question panel for an NPS, with the high and low label rows. */}

## Rules you can set

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they pick a number. |

Required is the whole of it. Every answer the scale can give is already inside its range, so there is nothing left to bound and the panel has no **Validation** row.

## Logic that works with it

The answer is a number, so conditions compare it as one: **is**, **is not**, **is more than**, **is at least**, **is less than**, **is at most**, **is between**, **is answered** and **is not answered**. That is how you build the usual follow-ups, one question for **is at most** 6 and another for **is at least** 9. It [scores](/logic/scoring) a number per scale point, with a row for every point from 0 to 10, and a point can be negative. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), either one pass for the answer they gave or as many passes as the number they chose. It is not a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source and nothing carries into it, because a rating is a measurement rather than a list of things. [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the number they picked.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is an **NPS gauge**: a dial carrying the score, from minus 100 to plus 100, with the three groups named under it as **Detractors** for 0 to 6, **Passives** for 7 and 8, and **Promoters** for 9 and 10. The score is the share of promoters less the share of detractors. **Bar chart** is the other entry in **Chart type**, and it shows how the score was earned, one bar per point from 0 to 10.

In every export the question becomes one column holding the number that respondent picked.
