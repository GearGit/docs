Smiley rating draws a row of faces, from the unhappiest on the left to the happiest on the right, and a respondent picks the one that matches how they feel. The answer is stored as a number, so a row of faces still gives you something you can average and branch on.

{/* screenshot: A smiley rating question as a respondent sees it, five faces in a row with a caption under each end. */}

## When to use it

- You are asking about feeling rather than judgment: how a visit went, how a message landed, how they feel about a change.
- Your respondents are not all reading the same language well, or are answering on a phone, and a face is quicker to read than a sentence.
- You want a short scale on purpose, because three or five steps is as fine a distinction as the question deserves.

**Consider instead:** [Star rating](/questions/ratings/star-rating) when quality is being judged and you want up to ten points, [Thumbs up or down](/questions/ratings/thumbs-up-down) when two answers are enough, [Likert](/questions/ratings/likert) when you want the points named in words, [NPS](/questions/ratings/nps) when you want the standard 0 to 10 recommendation score.

## Add it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a gap on the canvas">
    On the **Build** tab, hover between two questions and click **Add**. A gap sits above the first question, between every pair, and after the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick Smiley Rating">
    In **Add to survey**, type to filter and click **Smiley Rating**, under **Rating**. The question lands where the gap was with five faces already drawn and no end captions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the question">
    Type the question on the canvas. Choose the scale and write the end captions in the panel beside it.

    {/* screenshot: The smiley rating question on the builder canvas, edited in place. */}
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Select the question on the canvas and the panel beside the rail shows everything you can set.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Scale** | How many faces the row holds, **3** or **5**. It arrives on 5. The faces change with the count, so a three point scale is an unhappy face, a neutral one and a happy one. |
| **High label** | A caption for the happiest face. On a scale this short the caption sits under the face it belongs to rather than at the end of the row. |
| **Low label** | A caption for the unhappiest face, under the other end. |

Both captions start empty, so a question with nothing typed into them shows faces and no words.

Responses already collected stay attached to the version they were given on, so changing **Scale** later does not rewrite them. See [editing a live survey](/share/editing-a-live-survey).

{/* screenshot: The question panel for a smiley rating, with the scale row and the two end label rows. */}

## Rules you can set

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [Required](/rules/required) | They cannot move on until they pick a face. |

Required is the whole of it. Every answer the scale offers is already inside its range, so there is nothing left to bound and the panel has no **Validation** row. Left optional, a respondent can clear the answer by clicking the face they already picked.

## Logic that works with it

The answer is a number, counting from 1 at the unhappy end, so conditions compare it as one: **is**, **is not**, **is more than**, **is at least**, **is less than**, **is at most**, **is between**, **is answered** and **is not answered**. It [scores](/logic/scoring) points per scale point, one row per face, and a point can be negative, so an unhappy answer can cost marks. It can [drive a loop](/logic/loops), either one pass for the answer they gave or as many passes as the number they chose. It is not a [carry-forward](/logic/carry-forward) source and nothing carries into it, because a rating is a measurement rather than a list of things worth asking about again. [Piping](/logic/piping) it into later wording prints the number they picked.

## How it shows in results

Per question the default is a **Bar chart**, one bar per face, counting the respondents who picked it. **Histogram** and **Pie chart** are the other two entries in **Chart type**.

In every export the question becomes one column holding the number that respondent picked.
